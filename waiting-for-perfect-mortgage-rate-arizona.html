Why Waiting for the Perfect Rate Could Cost You More Than You Think

By Sia Klaibua  ·  September 22, 2026  ·  6 min read  ·  NMLS# 2823225

Every Week I Hear the Same Thing

Buyers tell me they are waiting for rates to come down before they purchase. I understand the instinct — 7.54% is a real number and it produces a real payment. But when I run the actual math on waiting versus buying now, the results consistently surprise people.

What Waiting One Year Actually Costs

Consider two buyers making the same decision today on a $455,000 home — the current Phoenix metro median. Buyer A purchases today at 7.54% with 10% down. Monthly principal and interest: approximately $2,870. Buyer B waits one year, hoping for a lower rate. That same home, after 5% appreciation — which is within the forecast range for most Phoenix submarkets — now costs $477,750. Even if rates drop to 6.5%, Buyer B's monthly payment is approximately $2,725 — a savings of just $145 per month. But Buyer B paid $22,750 more for the home and missed 12 months of equity building. It takes more than 13 years of that monthly savings just to break even on the wait.

When Rates Drop, Everything Gets Harder

The Arizona Association of Realtors has noted that every half-point drop in mortgage rates prices in approximately 40,000 additional Phoenix metro households. Every buyer waiting on the sidelines floods back into the market simultaneously — and prices respond accordingly. The buyers who purchased during the higher-rate period then refinance, keep their lower purchase price, and benefit from both sides of the equation.

You can refinance a rate. You cannot go back and buy the same home at last year's price.

Equity Starts on Day One

Every mortgage payment builds equity. Every month you rent, you build zero. The principle of marrying the house and dating the rate is real — it is not a sales line. It is the math of how real estate wealth is built across every rate cycle in modern history. The buyers who acted at 8%, 10%, and even 14% rates in previous decades are the ones who built generational wealth.

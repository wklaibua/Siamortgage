7% Feels High — But History Tells a Different Story

By Sia Klaibua  ·  September 21, 2026  ·  6 min read  ·  NMLS# 2823225

The Rate That Feels Impossible — But Is Not

When clients see today's rate of 7.54%, many feel like they are being asked to buy a home under conditions that are uniquely difficult — a punishment for bad timing. The truth, when you look at the full history of mortgage rates in America, is almost exactly the opposite. Today's rate is not an aberration. What came before it was.

What Buyers Paid in the 1980s

From 1980 to 1990, the 30-year fixed mortgage rate averaged between 10% and 18%. In October 1981, it hit an all-time high of 18.63%. Buyers at that rate were paying nearly two and a half times what today's buyers pay in interest — on top of home prices that, while far lower in absolute dollars, represented comparable income multiples to what buyers face today. And yet they bought homes. They had families in those homes. They built equity. Those homes are worth five to ten times what they paid. The rate felt impossible at the time. The decision to buy anyway was transformative.

The COVID Anomaly That Distorted Everything

The 2.5% to 3.5% rates of 2020 and 2021 were not normal. They were an emergency intervention — the Federal Reserve's response to a global pandemic that shut down the world economy. That era of rates was extraordinary, temporary, and deeply distorting. Buyers who chased those ultra-low rates paid record-high home prices to get them. A home in Phoenix that was $350,000 in 2019 was $550,000 by 2021. The low rate came with a price premium that often erased the payment advantage entirely.

From 1971 to 2023, the average 30-year fixed mortgage rate was 7.74%. Today's rate of 7.54% is slightly below that 52-year average. It is not a crisis. It is a return to normal.

What the History Actually Tells Us

The consistent pattern across every rate cycle in modern American real estate history is this: the buyers who waited for the perfect rate rarely found it, and often paid more for the same home when they finally acted. The buyers who purchased at whatever rate was available — built equity, refinanced when rates improved, and accumulated wealth over time — consistently outperformed those who waited.

If you are ready to buy in Arizona, the rate is what it is. And based on 52 years of data, it is not as extraordinary as it feels. The purchase price you lock in today is what matters most over a long holding period.

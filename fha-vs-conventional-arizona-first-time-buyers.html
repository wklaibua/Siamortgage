FHA vs Conventional — Which Loan Is Right for Arizona First-Time Buyers?

By Sia Klaibua  ·  September 23, 2026  ·  6 min read  ·  NMLS# 2823225

The Most Important Decision Before You Make an Offer

Most first-time buyers spend weeks choosing between homes and less than an hour choosing their loan. That is exactly backwards. The loan you use — FHA or conventional — affects your monthly payment, your long-term cost, your ability to remove mortgage insurance, and in some cases your competitiveness as a buyer.

FHA Loans — Built for Accessibility

FHA loans are backed by the Federal Housing Administration and designed specifically to help buyers with lower credit scores or limited down payment savings access homeownership. The FHA 30-year rate as of September 30, 2026 is 7.17% per Mortgage News Daily — lower than the conventional rate, which matters on a monthly basis.

The key features: minimum credit score of 580 with 3.5% down; scores between 500 and 579 may qualify with 10% down; more flexible debt-to-income guidelines. The trade-off is mortgage insurance. FHA requires an upfront premium of 1.75% of the loan amount — on a $430,000 loan that is $7,525 added to your balance — plus a monthly premium that for most buyers with less than 10% down remains for the life of the loan.

Conventional Loans — Better Long-Term for Stronger Profiles

Conventional loans follow Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac guidelines. The 30-year conventional rate as of September 30, 2026 is 7.54%. Minimum credit score of 620, with the best rates reserved for 740 and above. Down payments as low as 3% through HomeOne for first-time buyers. Private mortgage insurance is required below 20% down — but critically, it can be removed once you reach 20% equity. The 2026 conforming limit in Maricopa County is $832,750.

The right loan is not the one with the lowest rate. It is the one that works best for your credit profile, your down payment, and your long-term plan.

Which One Is Right for You

Use this as a starting framework: credit score below 620 — FHA is likely your best path. Credit score 620 to 739 — compare both side by side because the lower FHA rate may offset the ongoing MIP. Credit score 740 and above — conventional almost always produces the lower long-term cost. Purchasing above $832,750 — conventional jumbo financing is required regardless.
